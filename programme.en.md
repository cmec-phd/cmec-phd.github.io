The PhD programme in **Computational Electroacoustic Music Composition, Generative Algorithmic Models and Computational Complexity** is the three-year doctoral programme of the Conservatorio di Musica "N. Piccinni" of Bari. It is based in the School of Electronic Music and coordinated by [Francesco Scagliola](/en/people/francesco-scagliola/). The current cycle is the XLI (academic year 2025/2026).

## The programme

The programme starts from the premise that electroacoustic composition can be both an artistic practice and a field of research. Algorithmic models, mathematical formalisms and computational tools play a double role: they are means of composing and, at the same time, objects to be investigated, described and tested.

The programme sits where music theory, acoustics and perception, computer science and complex systems meet. Its aim is to train composer-researchers who can build their own theoretical and technical tools, assess their results critically and share them with the scientific and artistic community.

## Research lines

Research in the XLI cycle approaches musical material on complementary levels: the combinatorics of pitch-class sets as a computable structure, roughness as a bridge between symbolic structure and perception, the ontological modelling of musical time, and the algorithmic generation of trajectories on lattices as a compositional space.

## Artistic research and method

Doctoral work is research-led. Each project leads to an artistic or design outcome (a composition, a system, a software tool) accompanied by a written account of its methods, research process and references. The programme encourages international mobility and ongoing exchange with the computer music research community.

The Doctoral Board brings together expertise from the Conservatorio (composition, electroacoustics, performance of electroacoustic music, music theory and perception) with colleagues from the University of Bari Aldo Moro and the Polytechnic University of Bari. Board members and doctoral candidates are listed on the [People](/en/people/) page.

## Dissemination

The programme's results are presented at national and international venues in computer music and artistic research: the Colloquium on Music Informatics ([From Local Kernels to Global Form](/en/research/vitucci2026kernels/) and [Memory as Transformation: LETHE](/en/research/vitucci2026lethe/), CIM 2026), the International Csound Conference ([Opening Mind by Opening Architecture](/en/research/vitucci2024opening/), ICSC 2024; [One Timeline, Many Renderings](/en/research/vitucci2026timeline/), ICSC 2026), the journal *Ex Chordis* ([In Human Memories](/en/research/vitucci2024memories/), 2024) and DISCo AFAM 2026 in Terni, where the doctoral candidates present their work on lattice trajectories and on the modelling of musical time.

The programme also engages a wider public, through events such as *La Musica, da Pitagora all'Intelligenza Artificiale* at the Polytechnic University of Bari (2025), *Musica e/è Scienza* at the Conservatorio (2025), *E.C.O. – Elettronica Condivisa Online* (2026) and European Researchers' Night (ERN 2026). Publications and events are collected in the [Research](/en/research/) and [News & Events](/en/news/) sections.

## Admission

Admission is by public competition, open to candidates holding a second-level academic diploma in Electronic Music or an equivalent degree, and is based on the evaluation of a personal research project. Calls, rankings and official notices are published on the [programme page of the Conservatorio website](https://www.consba.it/it/3695/) (in Italian).
